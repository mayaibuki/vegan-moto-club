## 1. Schema

## 2. Queue
Query the data source (SQL mode) for rows where URL is set and any of these hold:
- "Name of product" is empty, is "Unknown product", starts with "User Suggestion -", or starts with "http" (a URL pasted as the title)
- Description is empty or shorter than 200 characters
- Description starts with "The " and contains " is a " followed by a lowercase category word (jackets, gloves, pants, boots, racing suits, protection, other, street wear, piece of riding gear; these category words are legacy template text from a retired script; legacy rows still exist), or contains "designed for riders who want synthetic, animal-free construction"
- Brand, Category, Riding style, Season or Vegan Verified is empty
Order by priority, then by the system createdTime column ascending (the property called "Created time" is actually a last-edited timestamp; do not use it): first rows with a placeholder or empty title, an empty description or a template description (these are hidden from the site), then rows with a description under 200 characters, then rows that only have empty fields. Take at most 10 rows. Rows whose Description already has 200 or more characters of non-template prose and whose only gap is Season are processed as field-only rows: the writer fills Season from the inference rule in step 4 and the description is not rewritten. Rows whose only gap is Riding style are processed the same way, using the Riding style inference rule in step 4.
## 2b. Duplicate check (whole database, every run)
Before processing the queue, check the entire data source for duplicates, not only the queued rows. Run SQL over all rows and group by: (a) the URL lowercased with the query string and trailing slash removed; (b) the last path segment of that URL (the product handle), which catches the same product filed under different collection paths or locales; (c) Brand plus "Name of product" lowercased with ®, ™, spaces, hyphens, periods and apostrophes removed. Any group with 2 or more rows is a possible duplicate. Size or color variants of one product count as duplicates (the Held Safer SRX jacket existed twice with different size variants in the URL). A jacket and its matching pants, or a men's and women's version, are separate products, not duplicates. List every duplicate group in the report under "Possible duplicates" with both Notion URLs and createdTime, and say which is older. Never delete or merge rows; do not enrich the newer copy.

## 3. Research (gear-writer per product)
The writer fetches the product URL with WebFetch (or curl via Bash if WebFetch is blocked; for Shopify stores the /products/<handle>.json endpoint is reliable; revitsport.com root prices are EUR, /en-us/ is USD). It extracts: real product name (page h1), maker brand (not retailer), price and currency, category, gender, materials from the spec sheet, CE rating (EN 17092 class AAA/AA/A/B/C, EN 13594 level 1/2 for gloves, EN 13634 for boots, EN 1621 armor level 1/2), waterproofing technology, riding use, season cues, and every mention of animal materials (leather, suede, nubuck, wool, down, fur, sheepskin, kangaroo, goat, cow, silk, merino, cashmere) or explicit vegan claims. It records the sentence that supports each claim; a field without a supporting quote is left empty. Before launching writers, fetch one dated EUR to USD and one GBP to USD rate (frankfurter.dev or open.er-api.com) and pass them to every writer; writers convert with those rates, rounded to the nearest dollar, and record the rate used.

## 3b. Community research (gear-researcher per product)
The researcher searches Reddit, ADVrider and other rider forums, YouTube reviews and retailer review tabs for the product. It labels every source as independent, affiliate or sponsored, or unknown, with the reason. Sponsored or affiliate content and brand-supplied units count as the maker's claim. It reports a consensus only when 2 or more independent sources agree, notes disputes, and gives a confidence level. It looks for the negative threads too (sizing, zippers, durability, waterproofing failures). When nothing independent exists, it says so.

## 4. Field mapping
- Brand: exact option match. If the maker is not an option, leave Brand empty, make sure the maker is named in the first sentence of the description, and list it under "Add these Brand options" in the report; the owner adds it and the row is picked up next run.
- Category: one or more options, by the rule in `docs/category-rules.md`. (1) Armor, armor pockets or a CE garment class (EN 17092): file by garment, upper body = Jackets (armored hoodies, overshirts and riding sweaters included), legs = Pants, one-piece = Racing Suits. (2) Standalone armor, airbags and armored base layers = Protection; armored leggings may carry Pants + Protection. (3) Gloves and glove liners = Gloves; footwear = Boots. (4) Insulating mid-layer jackets and pants = Jackets / Pants. (5) Everything else = Other, and only Other, never a second category: rain over-garments without armor, unarmored base layers and undersuits, thermal base layers, non-protective tees and casual clothing including unarmored jeans, helmets, luggage and bags, accessories. Sentence one of the description names the product type ("tank bag", "rain jacket"), never the word "Other". Never create options.
- Luggage, helmets and accessories (green gate defaults): Gender Unisex, Level of Protection "Not protective", all three seasons unless the page says otherwise. Do not infer Season from words like "insulated" or "mesh pocket", and Riding style follows what the maker says the item is for (default Commute / Street, plus Adventure / Touring for luggage only if the page supports it). Rain over-garments without armor: Not protective; Level of Waterproof follows the page.
- Gender: from page sizing. Both men's and women's sizing means Unisex.
- Level of Protection: AAA with CE level 2 armor (racing-grade gear) = Most protective; AA with CE level 2 armor = Highly protective; CE level 1 armor caps the rating at Moderately protective whatever the abrasion class, and class A = Moderately protective; level 2 armor with no abrasion class (gloves, boots, standalone protectors) = Highly protective; AAA with no armor level stated = Highly protective; AA with no armor level stated = Moderately protective; armor mentioned without a level, or B or C = Slightly protective; no protection = Not protective.
- Level of Waterproof: by membrane name (D-Dry, Gore-tex, Hydratex, Drystar, NextDry); another membrane = Waterproof; DWR or "water repellent" only = Water resistant; vented summer item = Not waterproof. Leave empty when the page says nothing.
- Riding style: always fill it; do not ask the owner. Use explicit product copy when the page names a use (touring, adventure, urban, city, commuting, sport, track, race, off-road, enduro, MX). Otherwise decide from the construction with this table and record the page line used as `riding_style_basis`, marked "inferred": Adventure / Touring = waterproof-membrane or lined gear (Gore-Tex or other membrane gloves, jackets, pants and boots; removable or thermal liners; long cuffs; multi-season venting). Commute / Street = short-cuff casual gloves without a membrane, jeans and casual pants, hoodies and casual jackets, riding shoes and sneakers. Sport / Canyons = sport-cut gear with hard armor (hard knuckle shells, sliders, pre-curved sport fit, sport jackets with hard armor). Racing / Trackdays = one-piece suits, race boots and race gauntlets. Off-roading = MX or enduro gear (MX boots, jerseys, pants, enduro jackets). Tie-breaks, applied in order so a value is always chosen: (1) explicit copy beats inference; (2) Racing and Off-roading beat everything else when their cues are present; (3) a waterproof membrane or thermal liner makes it Adventure / Touring; (4) hard sport armor makes it Sport / Canyons; (5) anything else is Commute / Street. Two values are allowed only when the page names both uses. Site menus and breadcrumbs are never cues. Leave the field empty only when the product page cannot be read at all, and say so in the report.
- Season: always fill it; do not ask the owner. Use explicit product copy when the page names a season. Otherwise decide from the construction and record the page line used as `season_basis`, marked "inferred": Summer = mesh, perforated or vented summer items, and thin gloves with no membrane, lining or insulation; Winter = insulation (Thinsulate, PrimaLoft, fleece), thermal liner or heated elements; Mid season = waterproof membrane or removable liner without insulation; add Winter when it is also insulated, and add Summer when it also has large mesh vent panels; liners, base layers and windproof layers = the season the maker names, else Mid season and Winter. Tie-breaks, applied in order: (1) explicit copy beats inference; (2) insulation means Winter is included; (3) a membrane means Mid season is included; (4) no membrane and no insulation means Summer. Site menus and breadcrumbs are never cues. Leave the field empty only when the product page cannot be read at all, and say so in the report.
- Vegan Verified: "Confirmed Vegan by maker" whenever the official product page says vegan or animal-free in the maker's own words, including a bare "Vegan" tag in the spec or sustainability block, unless something else indicates a material of animal origin in the product itself (an animal material in the spec, materials list, care label or a bundled part). Negated or comparison mentions ("leather-free label", "leather alternative", "fits like leather pants"), site chrome and SEO keyword lists in image alt text are not such indications. "Verified Vegan by AI" when there is no maker claim but every listed material is synthetic and no animal term appears anywhere on the page; otherwise "Waiting for confirmation as Vegan". When the product's own spec, materials list, care label or a bundled part names an animal material, write "Not vegan", name the material and where it is in the description, and flag the row in red. Never write "Not vegan" from site chrome, cross-sells, alt-text keyword lists or negated mentions.
- Price: USD number, the price a buyer pays today. When the page shows a sale price, store the sale price if the discount is permanent (outlet or clearance tags such as "OutletUS", a reduced price with no end date, or no compare-at price in the store feed). Keep the regular price when the discount is temporary (Black Friday, Cyber Monday, holiday or seasonal sale, a stated end date or countdown). Record which case applied in the report.
- Materials: only existing single options that the spec sheet supports.
Only write properties the writer supported with a quote and the verifier passed. Omit the rest.

## 5. Description
gear-writer returns the draft and gear-verifier has passed it: 4 to 6 sentences of plain prose, under 900 characters, no bullets, headers, emojis or internal notes. Sentence one names the product, maker and category and what it is for, under 160 characters (it becomes the page's meta description). The rest covers construction and materials, protection with the exact standard, fit and sizing notes, and one honest limitation or "worth knowing" detail from the page. When the researcher reports medium or high confidence, one sentence reports what owners say, attributed as owner reports and naming disputes; with low or no confidence, nothing about reception. Never cite a sponsored source. No em dashes, no en dashes, no double hyphens, no hype, no claims not on the page, none of the words or constructions banned in docs/brand-voice.md. Do not say vegan unless the maker does; say "animal-free materials listed" when that is what the spec supports.
Then run the local humanizer skill (Skill tool, skill "humanizer") in embedded mode on each draft: output only the final text, and it must not add any fact that is not in the writer's evidence. Then review the batch once over all humanized descriptions with docs/brand-voice.md as the guide. Use the marketing brand-review skill (Skill tool, skill "marketing:brand-review") when it is available and do not answer its follow-up menu. When it is not available (cloud), review the batch yourself against docs/brand-voice.md and report findings in the same High/Medium/Low severity format with before/after fixes. Apply the before/after fixes for High and Medium findings. A description with any unresolved High finding is not written; the row goes in the report under "Blocked by brand review".
Final check per description: 4 to 6 sentences, under 900 characters, no "—" or "–", every fact traceable to the writer's evidence or the researcher's consensus.
